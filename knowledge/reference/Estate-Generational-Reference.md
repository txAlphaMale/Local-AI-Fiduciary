# Estate & Generational Wealth Transfer — Reference (2026, Texas MFJ household)

_Corpus doc for the Fiduciary knowledge RAG (G2 companion). Values verified 2026-07 against the app's grounding registry — the live source of truth is AUDITS → GROUNDING (`/api/grounding`). Educational reference, not legal advice; estate documents require a licensed Texas attorney._

## Federal exemption regime (2026, post-OBBBA)
- Estate/gift basic exclusion: **$15,000,000 per person** ($30,000,000 married shield with portability), permanently set by OBBBA and inflation-indexed after 2026. Top estate/gift tax rate above the shield: **40%**.
- **GST exemption: $15,000,000 per person — NOT portable** between spouses. Generation-skipping transfers (to grandchildren or skip trusts) must be allocated deliberately.
- **Portability requires filing Form 706** at the first spouse's death, even when no tax is due. A sub-exemption estate that skips the 706 forfeits the deceased spouse's unused exclusion (DSUE).
- Annual gift-tax exclusion: **$19,000 per donee per donor (2026)** — a married couple can give $38,000 per recipient per year with zero filing. **529 superfunding:** 5 years of exclusions at once (up to $95,000/donor/beneficiary, elected on Form 709).

## Basis: the dominant planning lens for a sub-exemption estate
For a household far below the shield, the estate-tax rate is 0% — so **basis management IS estate planning**:
- **§1014 step-up:** assets owned at death take a fair-market-value basis; all unrealized capital gain is erased. Appreciated assets are cheapest to transfer at death.
- **Lifetime gifts carry the donor's basis** (§1015 carryover). Gifting appreciated stock hands the recipient the embedded gain. Prefer gifting **cash or high-basis assets**; hold low-basis assets to death.
- **Texas community property double step-up:** at the FIRST spouse's death, BOTH halves of community property receive a full §1014 step-up (IRC §1014(b)(6)) — not just the decedent's half as in common-law states. Keeping taxable brokerage assets titled as community property preserves this; separate-property titling or moving to a common-law state can forfeit half the step-up.
- **Primary residence:** §121 excludes $250k single / $500k MFJ of gain on sale during life (2-of-5-year use test); at death §1014 wipes the remaining gain entirely. The choice between selling late in life vs holding to death is a §121-vs-§1014 comparison.

## Titling & beneficiary hygiene (what actually moves assets)
- Beneficiary designations (401k/IRA/HSA/life insurance) and account titling **override the will**. An outdated designation defeats every other document.
- **Texas Transfer-on-Death Deed** passes the homestead outside probate, revocable, and does NOT sacrifice the §1014 step-up. TOD/POD registrations do the same for brokerage/bank accounts.
- Core document set and staleness: will, durable power of attorney, medical POA + HIPAA release, directive to physicians. Review after every move, marriage/divorce, birth/death, or ~5 years.

## Wealth-transfer avenue ranking for THIS profile (sub-exemption, generational goal)
1. Hold appreciated assets to death (free basis erase, $0 tax) — the default.
2. Annual-exclusion gifting of cash/high-basis assets ($19k×2 per recipient) — moves growth out of the estate early with no filing.
3. 529 superfund for education-destined dollars (tax-free growth + [state-tax n/a in TX]; SECURE 2.0 allows up to $35k lifetime rollover of leftover 529 to the beneficiary's Roth IRA, subject to 15-year account age and annual Roth limits).
4. Roth conversions during low-bracket years — heirs inherit Roth income-tax-free (10-year rule still applies, but with no tax inside the window vs a traditional IRA's taxable RMD drain).
5. Trust structures (SLAT/IDGT/GRAT) only become tax-relevant if the projected estate crosses the (growing) exemption — the app's estate module projects the crossing year.

## Trust structures for exemption-crossing estates (SLAT / IDGT / GRAT / dynasty trusts)
Item 5 above flags these as only becoming tax-relevant once a household's projected estate crosses the (growing) exemption — GAP-11 in the app's own backlog. Structural mechanics (stable IRC provisions, not annually-indexed figures) for the education tier:

- **SLAT (Spousal Lifetime Access Trust):** one spouse (the grantor) irrevocably gifts assets into a trust for the OTHER spouse's benefit. Removes the assets and all future growth from the grantor's estate, while the grantor's household retains indirect access through the beneficiary spouse. GST exemption can be allocated so trust assets also skip the grandchildren's generation. Two real risks: (1) **reciprocal trust doctrine** — if both spouses create near-identical SLATs for each other, the IRS can "uncross" them and treat each grantor as still controlling their own trust, unwinding the estate benefit (structures must differ meaningfully in terms/timing/beneficiaries); (2) **divorce risk** — if the marriage ends, the grantor spouse permanently loses indirect access to trust assets even though they gave them up for the OTHER spouse's benefit, not a stranger's.
- **IDGT (Intentionally Defective Grantor Trust):** irrevocable and OUT of the grantor's estate for estate-tax purposes, but deliberately structured to remain "defective" (still the grantor) for INCOME-tax purposes — the grantor personally pays the trust's income tax. That tax payment is itself a further gift-tax-free transfer to the trust (the trust grows undiminished by tax drag, and the grantor's own estate shrinks by the tax paid — a "stealth" additional wealth transfer with no gift-tax cost). Classic technique: the grantor SELLS appreciated assets to the IDGT in exchange for a promissory note at the §7520 rate — an "asset freeze," since all growth above the note's interest rate accrues inside the trust, outside the estate. Pairs naturally with GST exemption allocation for a multi-generation ("dynasty") structure.
- **GRAT (Grantor Retained Annuity Trust):** the grantor transfers assets into the trust but retains an annuity payment stream for a fixed term; whatever remains at the term's end passes to the named remainder beneficiaries. If the trust's actual investment growth beats the IRS §7520 hurdle rate used to value the retained annuity, the EXCESS passes to beneficiaries gift-tax-free — a "zeroed-out" GRAT can be structured so the taxable gift at funding is close to $0. Low-downside technique: if growth underperforms the hurdle, the annuity simply returns everything to the grantor (the assets were never really at risk of a wasted gift). Key limitation: due to the "ETIP" (estate tax inclusion period) rule, GST exemption generally can't be allocated efficiently to a GRAT while the annuity term is running, so a GRAT alone is NOT a dynasty-trust tool — it's often paired with a subsequent sale of the remainder into an IDGT once the GRAT term ends.
- **Dynasty trust:** any of the above (most commonly an IDGT) can be structured to last multiple generations rather than terminating at the next generation, so long as state law allows it — some states have abolished or extended the rule against perpetuities specifically to enable this (a state-of-formation/situs choice, distinct from the household's state of RESIDENCE the app tracks — see `states.py`'s TOD/community-property facts for the residence-side analog). Funded with GST-exemption-allocated assets, distributions can skip a transfer-tax layer at every generation the trust spans.
- **GST allocation mechanics:** exemption is $15,000,000 per person for 2026, NOT portable between spouses (already noted above). Certain transfers to a "GST trust" (as defined in §2632(c)) get AUTOMATIC allocation of exemption unless the grantor affirmatively opts out — useful as a backstop, but most practitioners file an affirmative Form 709 election for deliberate control rather than relying on the automatic rule, since automatic allocation can waste exemption on a trust that didn't need it or under-allocate to one that did.

None of this is a drafting substitute — every structure above needs a licensed estate-planning attorney to implement correctly (state of trust situs, trustee selection, funding mechanics, and coordination with the marital deduction/portability election all matter and are fact-specific). This section exists so CHAT can explain what these terms MEAN and roughly WHEN they become relevant; the app's estate module's own crossing-year projection is the trigger to have that attorney conversation, not a specific dollar threshold in this doc.
