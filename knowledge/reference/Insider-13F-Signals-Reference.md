# Insider Trading (Form 4) & 13F Whale-Tracking — Frameworks Reference (2026)

_Corpus doc for the Fiduciary knowledge RAG (G15 companion). Concepts behind `insider.py`/`inst13f.py` and their surfacing in research/theses. Both signals are explicitly CONTEXT-ONLY in this app — not standalone buy/sell triggers. Not investment advice._

## Form 4: insider open-market trading
- **Form 4** is the SEC filing corporate insiders (officers, directors, and holders of more than 10% of a company's stock — "10% owners") must file within two business days of a reportable transaction in their own company's stock. It discloses the transaction date, type (coded), share count, and price.
- **Only open-market transactions are a real signal.** Transaction code **P** = open-market purchase, code **S** = open-market sale — these are the codes worth weighting, because they represent an insider voluntarily using their own money in the open market. Most OTHER codes on a Form 4 are NOT discretionary buy/sell signals: option/RSU exercises, grants, and 10b5-1 pre-scheduled trading-plan sales are routine compensation or diversification mechanics that happen regardless of the insider's view on the stock, and lumping them in with code P/S activity dilutes or actively misleads a "net insider buying" signal.
- **Reading net insider activity**: aggregating P vs. S transaction VALUE across a filing window gives a net-buy percentage and a count of distinct buyers vs. sellers. A cluster of MULTIPLE different insiders (not just one, and not the CEO's routine diversification sale) buying in the open market in a short window is a materially stronger signal than a single insider's single transaction — insiders sell for many personal reasons (diversification, taxes, a house purchase) but rarely buy in the open market for reasons unrelated to their view of the company.

## 13F: institutional "whale" tracking
- **Form 13F-HR** is a quarterly filing required of institutional investment managers with over $100 million in qualifying assets, disclosing their U.S. equity long positions as of the prior quarter-end. It's filed within 45 days after quarter-end.
- **The lag is the central limitation**: a 13F position reported today reflects where that manager stood UP TO 45 days ago plus however far into the current quarter you are — up to a ~135-day-stale snapshot by the time the NEXT 13F would supersede it. A manager can have completely exited a position the reader is excitedly buying because of a 13F that still shows them "in." Treat 13F holdings as historical record, not current positioning.
- **13F discloses LONG equity positions only** — no short positions, no derivatives in most cases, no cash, no non-U.S.-listed holdings, and no context on WHY a position was taken or changed. A manager "adding" to a position says nothing about their conviction level, time horizon, or whether it's a core holding or a small tactical trade relative to their total book.
- **Reading quarter-over-quarter change**: comparing share counts between consecutive 13F filings classifies a position as new / added / trimmed / exited / unchanged (a small tolerance band avoids flagging trivial rebalancing noise as a real change). "New" and "exited" are the highest-information categories; "added"/"trimmed" percentage changes matter more when they're large relative to the manager's typical position sizing, which requires knowing the manager's overall portfolio concentration to judge properly.

## Why both are context-only, not triggers
- Neither signal has a reliable, back-tested predictive edge on its own at the individual-filing level — insider and institutional activity are lagging, incomplete, and driven by many non-signal reasons (diversification, redemptions, mandate changes, tax-loss harvesting on the institutional side). They're most useful as ONE input alongside fundamentals, valuation, and a thesis' own exit triggers — corroborating or contradicting an existing view — rather than as a standalone reason to act. This app surfaces them exactly that way: attached to research and theses, not as independent alerts.
